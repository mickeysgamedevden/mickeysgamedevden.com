Shoot'em ups have not been my favorite video games genre for a long time. There were Xevious and Scramble, which left some of my earliest memories of the arcade in the 80', but on home consoles and home computers, other genres made a stronger impression. The games from my early childhood, on a Phillips Videopac, felt eighter extremely simplistic or rather enigmatic. On the Commodore 64 on the other hand, which means in my case a Commodore 128D in C64 mode, so many games and shooters were, as far as I remember, frustratingly difficult and not fun to play. I do remember Ocean's Rambo: First Blood Part II and Imagine's Green Beret, both run and gun games that featured a very captivating soundtrack. It is unsurprising that I never cared much about SEUCK, the Shoot-Em-Up Construction Kit, released in 1987, which had been favorably reviewed in the first edition of the German Power-Play Magazine, for which I had a subscription at the time. 

Things got interesting when, while working on a shooter prototype on PICO-8, I read about "Dezaemon" in the [Dezaemon Dimensions](https://www.magcloud.com/browse/issue/2337274) issue of Shmup\_Lyf, a magazine dedicated to the shoot'em up genre. Dezaemon is a series of Japanese shoot'em up games/building tools available since the Famicom days. A later version for PlayStation came with an additional disc with hundred user made sample games created with an earlier version of this tool. The relation between games made with one of the different Dezaemon and current [doujin](https://jisho.org/search/doujin) shmups can be well visible. 

This wealth of interesting games made me want to better understand the kind of blueprint the Dezaemon games provided for the different console platforms and generations. I tracked down the cartridges and discs for Famicom, Super Famicom, PlayStation, Nintendo 64 and Sega Saturn on eBay and am looking forward to diving deep into the Dezaemon Dimensions...
